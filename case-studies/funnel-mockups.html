Three takes on the same logic chain, all left‑to‑right: user needs feed bot needs, bot needs name a decision category, alternatives get weighed, and a requirement ships. Same content in all three — only the visual grammar changes. Hover any card to see where the click‑to‑expand layer would attach.

Option 1

Flow funnel

Sankey‑style ribbons that physically narrow left to right — widest at diffuse human needs, thinnest at the shipped requirement. Bot needs fan out, then converge back onto a single category, which is the one place the chain visibly branches and rejoins. Most infographic‑like of the three; needs a wide viewport, so it would want a stacked fallback on phones.

Option 2

Decision tracks

Four independent lanes, each a complete story you can read straight across in one pass. The funnel reads as a warming gradient rather than a narrowing shape — each lane starts on cool paper and ends in gold. Least visual machinery, most readable, and the only option that degrades cleanly to a single column on mobile.

Option 3

Funnel silhouette

The most literal reading of the brief: an actual wedge narrowing left to right, with the five stages as vertical slices inside it. Volume does the talking — the left edge is tall and crowded with needs, the right edge is a short stack of decisions. Strongest at a glance, tightest on space for text.
